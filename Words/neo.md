
1.  **Pseudomnesipathy** (soo-dom-nee-SIP-uh-thee)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *pseudo* (false) + *mnesis* (memory) + *pathos* (feeling, suffering).
    *   **Definition:** The unsettling, anxious feeling that a vivid and emotionally charged memory might be false, implanted, or digitally altered. It's the emotional fallout of deepfakes and misinformation applied to one's own past.
    *   **Example:** "After seeing an AI-generated photo from his childhood that never happened, he was struck by a wave of pseudomnesipathy, suddenly questioning the authenticity of all his memories."

2.  **Agaposcopy** (ah-gah-POSS-ko-pee)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *agape* (goodwill, love for humanity) + *skopos* (seeing, watching).
    *   **Definition:** The conscious, deliberate act of searching for and focusing on positive, uplifting, or hopeful news and content online; the opposite of "doomscrolling."
    *   **Example:** "Tired of the constant negativity, she dedicated ten minutes each morning to agaposcopy, actively seeking out stories of kindness, innovation, and progress."

3.  **Polyoptalgia** (pah-lee-op-TAL-jee-uh)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *poly* (many) + Latin *optare* (to choose) + Greek *algia* (pain).
    *   **Definition:** The mental fatigue, anxiety, or paralysis caused by being overwhelmed with an excessive number of choices, particularly in digital contexts like streaming services, online shopping, or app stores.
    *   **Example:** "Staring at the endless grid of movies on the streaming platform, he was hit by a severe bout of polyoptalgia and ended up watching nothing at all."

4.  **Ergotopia** (er-go-TOE-pee-uh)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *ergon* (work) + *topos* (place).
    *   **Definition:** A physical space, such as a home office, a library nook, or a public area, that has been perfectly designed and optimized by an individual for deep, focused digital work and creativity.
    *   **Example:** "He finally converted his spare room into a true ergotopia, complete with an ergonomic chair, perfect natural lighting, and a strict no-distractions rule."

5.  **Metaphonia** (met-uh-FOE-nee-uh)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *meta* (after, beyond) + *phone* (sound, voice).
    *   **Definition:** The lingering mental or emotional residue of a digital conversation (e.g., text, email, or video call) that continues to echo in one's thoughts and influence one's mood long after the interaction has officially ended.
    *   **Example:** "The argument on the group chat was over, but the metaphonia of the harsh words made it difficult for her to focus on her work for the rest of the day."

6.  **Paleonopsis** (pay-lee-oh-NOP-sis)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *palaios* (ancient) + *neo* (new) + *opsis* (view, sight).
    *   **Definition:** The profound experience of encountering an ancient idea, text, or piece of art that feels perfectly, startlingly new and immediately relevant to one's own life.
    *   **Example:** "Reading the stoic philosophy of Marcus Aurelius for the first time gave him a powerful sense of paleonopsis; the advice felt like it was written for him, this morning."

7.  **Kryptonomophobia** (krip-toe-no-mo-FOE-bee-uh)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *kryptos* (hidden) + *nomos* (law, rule) + *phobos* (fear).
    *   **Definition:** A specific modern anxiety or paranoia related to the unknown, invisible rules and algorithms that govern digital experiences (like social media feeds or search results) and shape one's choices and perceptions.
    *   **Example:** "After his ad recommendations became eerily specific, he developed a case of kryptonomophobia, constantly wondering how the platform was profiling and manipulating him."

8.  **Dataphile** (DAY-tuh-file)
    *   **Etymology:** Latin *datum* (a thing given) + Greek *philos* (loving).
    *   **Definition:** A person who has a deep love for data, statistics, and spreadsheets; someone who finds beauty and clarity in well-organized information and the patterns that can be derived from it.
    *   **Example:** "As a true dataphile, she found more joy and elegance in a perfectly structured database schema than in a famous painting."

9.  **Erotechy** (air-oh-TEK-ee)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *erotema* (question) + *techne* (art, skill).
    *   **Definition:** The craft or skillful art of designing effective and nuanced prompts to elicit desired, creative, or insightful responses from an artificial intelligence.
    *   **Example:** "In the age of generative AI, erotechy has become a more valuable professional skill than traditional coding for many creative roles."

10. **Sympsychia** (sim-PSY-kee-uh)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *syn* (together) + *psyche* (mind, soul).
    *   **Definition:** A state of collective consciousness or "group flow" that emerges from a group of people collaborating intensely online, where it feels as if they are thinking together as a single, unified entity.
    *   **Example:** "During the late-night brainstorming session on the shared document, the team achieved a state of sympsychia, with ideas flowing and building on each other seamlessly."

1.  **Graphopermanence** (graf-oh-PER-muh-nence)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *graphe* (writing) + Latin *permanens* (remaining, permanent).
    *   **Definition:** The subtle, persistent background anxiety that comes from knowing that one's past words, photos, and digital actions are permanently archived, searchable, and could resurface at any moment.
    *   **Example:** "Before posting the old, embarrassing photo, he felt a pang of graphopermanence, realizing it could be found by a future employer years from now."

2.  **Bradypraxis** (brad-ee-PRAK-sis)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *bradys* (slow) + *praxis* (an action, a practice).
    *   **Definition:** A considered, slow, and thoughtful opinion or action, deliberately formed and shared in contrast to the culture of immediate, reactive "hot takes."
    *   **Example:** "Instead of tweeting a knee-jerk reaction to the news, the philosopher practiced bradypraxis, waiting a day to gather her thoughts before writing a detailed, nuanced essay."

3.  **Chimerography** (k-eye-mer-OG-ruh-fee)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *Chimera* (a mythical creature made of parts of various animals) + *graphe* (writing, drawing).
    *   **Definition:** The distinct aesthetic of AI-generated art, characterized by its seamless blending of disparate or impossible elements, flawless textures, and an often dreamlike, slightly uncanny coherence.
    *   **Example:** "The painting had all the hallmarks of chimerography: hyper-realistic details fused into a surreal, logically impossible landscape."

4.  **Specteropia** (spek-ter-OH-pee-uh)
    *   **Etymology:** Latin *spectrum* (image, apparition) + Greek *ops* (eye).
    *   **Definition:** The specific type of eye strain and mental fatigue that comes from prolonged exposure to digital screens, characterized by a feeling of disconnectedness where the physical world appears less vivid than the digital one.
    *   **Example:** "After eight hours of video calls, his specteropia was so intense that the real world looked blurry and muted compared to his monitor."

5.  **Microphilia** (my-kro-FIL-ee-uh)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *mikros* (small) + *philia* (love, friendship).
    *   **Definition:** The profound sense of belonging and joy one feels upon discovering a small, highly specific, and passionate online community or subculture that perfectly matches a niche interest.
    *   **Example:** "She experienced a wave of microphilia when she found the online forum dedicated entirely to enthusiasts of 18th-century cartography tools."

6.  **Ephemeriscience** (eh-fem-er-EYE-ence)
    *   **Etymology:** Greek *ephemeros* (lasting for a day, fleeting) + Latin *scientia* (knowledge).
    *   **Definition:** Knowledge or information that exists almost exclusively in a fragile, digital form (e.g., on a single website, a niche wiki, or a social media thread) and is at constant risk of being lost forever due to link rot or deletion.
    *   **Example:** "The entire history of that early internet fan project was a form of ephemeriscience, stored on a single, now-defunct forum that no one archived."
